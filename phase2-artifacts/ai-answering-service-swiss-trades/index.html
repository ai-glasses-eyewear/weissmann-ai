Call Qualification Script Builder for Trades

A missed call is lost work — but a call answered badly is worse: a job booked for the wrong slot, an emergency treated as routine, a quote promised that can’t be kept. Pick your trade and get the six questions a good first call should ask, in order, with the urgency question worded for your work — then copy it as a starting script.

How to use this: it’s a checklist you can hand to whoever (or whatever) answers your phone — a new apprentice, a call service, or an AI assistant. The wording is a starting point to adapt in your own voice, not a fixed script to read robotically.

Then — and only then — set expectations on price

“Based on what you’ve described I can’t give you a fixed price over the phone — the person who comes out will confirm it before any work starts. What I can do is book the visit and make sure they arrive with the right parts.”

Never invent a number to win the booking. An honest “we’ll confirm on site” protects both sides; a made-up price is the fastest way to a bad review.

Note: a starting template, not legal, safety or pricing advice. The plumber and electrician urgency questions follow the guide; the others are worded on the same principle — ask one specific yes/no about the actual hazard, never judge by tone — and should be confirmed with the person who does the work.
