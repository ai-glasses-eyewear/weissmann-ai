Fallback-readiness self-audit

An AI receptionist depends on three things staying up at once: the caller's own connection, the calendar it queries mid-call, and the vendor's own platform. Each fails independently, and none is guaranteed to be up at every moment. Check what you have in place for each link, and see whether it currently fails toward a next step or toward silence.

The one rule this audit measures against

A receptionist does two jobs at once, and the safe default is not the same for both. The channel should fail open; the action should fail closed.

Channel → fail open Whatever broke behind the scenes, the caller is never met with silence, a dead line, or a repeated unhelpful sentence. There is always a next step: a message taken, a callback promised, a transfer to a human or voicemail.
Action → fail closed When the assistant can't verify what it needs — real availability, a confirmed slot, an accurate spelling — it doesn't act as if it had. An unverified booking is worse than no booking: nobody finds out until two people arrive for one slot.

Where your fallback chain stands

A receptionist is a chain of at least three links, each able to fail independently of the other two. A chain is only as resilient as its weakest link.
